# Short-form editing guide

## Story structure

A short should have a recognisable micro-arc:
1. **Hook:** show or state the reason to keep watching as early as the source allows.
2. **Setup:** give only the context needed to understand the moment.
3. **Escalation:** retain the exchange, build-up, or action that makes the payoff matter.
4. **Payoff:** let the result, punchline, reaction, or answer land.
5. **Exit:** end after the payoff; avoid an unnecessary outro.

Do not add a fake hook or rewrite a quote. If the best hook occurs later, start at the lead-in or use a clearly editorial title card without misrepresenting the source.

## Pacing

- Cut dead air when it does not carry meaning, but preserve comedic timing and suspense.
- Use J-cuts/L-cuts only when they preserve the actual sequence and do not confuse speaker attribution.
- Use punch-ins for emphasis, not on every sentence. Avoid repeated digital zoom that causes visible softness.
- Use hard cuts for conversational rhythm; use transitions only when a location/time change or visual idea benefits.
- Keep action continuous. Do not use speed changes that make speech unnatural or alter the event.
- Avoid intro logos. A branded end card is optional and should not consume the payoff.

## Vertical composition

- Default canvas: 9:16 portrait.
- Place the primary subject near a stable focal region and leave room for captions and platform UI.
- Prefer smart crop/subject tracking over a fixed center crop when the subject moves.
- If multiple speakers are important, use a split-screen or a controlled reframing sequence rather than cropping one person out.
- Do not stretch the image. If a vertical crop cannot preserve critical content, use a blurred background fill or a designed split layout.
- Respect source rotation metadata. Probe display aspect ratio, not only coded width/height.

## Visual hierarchy

1. Speaker/action and meaningful facial/avatar reaction.
2. Captions.
3. Supporting labels or context cards.
4. Decorative effects.

Keep text large enough for a phone and high contrast. Do not cover the speaker's mouth, face, game HUD, subtitles embedded in the source, or important action. Use a restrained palette and consistent typography.

## Motion graphics

Use motion to clarify state changes or reactions:
- Brief scale punch-in on a reveal or key reaction.
- Small emphasis shake only for a strong impact, not as a permanent camera effect.
- Simple chapter/context labels when the clip starts abruptly.
- Progress bars or counters only when they reflect real information.

Avoid excessive particles, emojis, fake chat comments, fabricated view counts, stock footage that implies a real event, or effects that distract from dialogue.

## Final pacing pass

Watch once at normal speed with audio. Watch a second time muted to inspect visual readability and framing. Listen once without looking at the screen to catch clipped words, abrupt audio changes, and music masking speech.
